The Golden Rules
  • Yellow first, always. Every graphic uses club yellow & green. Same fonts, same badge, every time.
  • Faces & emotion win. Players, fans, celebrations > empty pitches and logos.
  • Reply to everything. Every comment, every tag. Engagement is free reach.

Platform Playbook

Instagram flagship · @raynerslanefc

  • What wins: Reels of goals, celebrations and big tackles; carousel galleries from matchday; clean line-up graphics.
  • Format: Vertical 9:16 video for Reels; Stories for live, in-the-moment updates; the grid for your best polished posts.
  • Frequency: 3-5 posts a week, plus Stories every matchday.
  • Lane tip: Post the goal Reel within an hour of full-time while the buzz is high — speed beats polish.

TikTok

  • What wins: Raw, funny, behind-the-scenes clips — dressing-room scenes, mud, manager reactions, fan chants.
  • Format: Vertical video, 7-30 seconds, hook in the first second, on-screen captions, trending sounds.
  • Frequency: 2-4 clips a week. Quantity and personality matter more than production.
  • Lane tip: Non-league authenticity is your superpower — lean into the "real football" charm the big clubs can't fake.

X / Twitter @RaynersLaneFC

  • What wins: Live text commentary — line-ups, goals, half-time, full-time. This is your minute-by-minute feed.
  • Format: Short text posts; one clear photo or short clip; always tag the opponent and the league.
  • Frequency: Live-tweet every match; 3-5 posts on non-matchdays.
  • Lane tip: Pin your fixture or result for the week so first-time visitors instantly know the score.

Facebook

  • What wins: Longer posts, match reports, event notices and ticket reminders — your most loyal local audience lives here.
  • Format: Photo albums, link posts to the website, and shareable graphics. Reels cross-post well too.
  • Frequency: 3-4 posts a week; always post the fixture reminder a day or two before.
  • Lane tip: Share posts into local Harrow community groups — this is where you reach new bums-on-seats.

YouTube

  • What wins: Match highlights, goal-of-the-month, season reviews and player interviews — your long-form archive.
  • Format: Highlight packages 3-8 minutes; add Shorts (vertical, under 60s) to feed people back to the channel.
  • Frequency: One highlights video per home game when you can; Shorts whenever you have a clip.
  • Lane tip: Title clearly — "Rayners Lane v [OPPONENT] | Highlights | CCL Premier North" — so it's searchable for years.
Best Times to Post

Matchday live cadence

  • ~2 hours before: Line-up reveal + "we're here" Story from the ground.
  • Kick-off: "We're underway" post.
  • Every goal: Post within a minute or two — even just the score in text.
  • Half-time & full-time: Score graphic or quick text post each time.
  • Within the hour after: Goal Reel and Man of the Match while engagement is hottest.

Best windows on non-matchdays

  • Weekday evenings 6-9pm — people are home and scrolling. Your safest bet.
  • Lunchtime 12-1pm — a solid secondary window on weekdays.
  • Sunday evening — great for the weekend round-up and the week-ahead preview.
  • Avoid the dead zone: early mornings and mid-afternoon on workdays get the least reach.

Growing The Following

Practical, honest growth

  • Tag everyone relevant: the opponent, the Combined Counties League, players in the photo, and your sponsors. Tags put you in front of their followers too.
  • Collaborate: use Instagram's "Collab" feature on a new-signing post so it lands on the player's grid as well. Cross-post with the opponent's account on a good fixture.
  • Engage back: reply to comments, like the fans who tag you, and repost the best fan photos and videos to your Story. People follow clubs that talk back.
  • Lean on sponsors: ask Hanlon Dry Lining, McCafferty's, Higginson Stairs and The King Denham to share your posts to their audiences.
  • One clip, every platform: shoot a goal once, then post it to Instagram Reels, TikTok, a YouTube Short and Facebook. Maximum reach, minimum effort.
  • Make it a weekly habit: pick one slot a week to plan posts and reply to comments — consistency grows a following far more than the odd viral hit.
  • Use local hashtags & groups: #Harrow and local community Facebook groups bring in genuinely local new fans.
What to Measure (KPIs)

The few numbers that actually matter

  • Reach: how many separate accounts saw a post. The headline "did people see it?" number.
  • Saves & shares: the strongest signal a post was genuinely good — these spread your content far beyond your followers.
  • Follower growth: are you up week on week? Slow and steady is exactly what you want.
  • Profile taps: how many people clicked through to your profile — a sign a post made someone curious about the club.
  • Best-performing post: each month, find your top post and simply do more of that.

A simple monthly habit

On the first of each month, open the Instagram "Insights" tab, jot down followers, total reach and your top post into a notes file or spreadsheet. Five minutes. After a few months you'll see exactly what the Yellow Army loves — and you make more of it.

Tone of Voice & Handling the Tough Days

How The Lane sounds

  • Proud, warm and community-first. We're a 1933 club run by and for local people. Speak like a passionate fan, not a corporation.
  • Confident, never arrogant. Celebrate the lads hard, respect every opponent, and always thank the fans.
  • "We" and "us". The club, the team and the Yellow Army are one. Yellow & green runs through everything.

After a heavy loss

  • Keep it short, classy and forward-looking: "Not our day. We go again." Thank the travelling fans.
  • Never throw players under the bus, never make excuses, never sulk in public.

Refereeing controversy

  • Say nothing in the heat of the moment. Don't blame the officials online — it invites bans and bad feeling.
  • If you must address it, be calm and brief, then move on. Let the result speak.

What NOT to post

  • No digs at opponents, officials or other clubs. No politics. No arguing in the comments.
  • Nothing about injuries, transfers or team news before the club has confirmed it.
  • No blurry, careless graphics with the wrong score or a misspelt name — check before you post.

Reply etiquette

  • Be friendly and quick. Thank people for support, answer questions about entry and kick-off times.
  • Don't feed trolls — a like or a polite one-liner, then leave it. Never argue.
  • If a comment is abusive, hide or delete it and move on. Protect the warmth of the page.

💰 Business Playbook
From one matchday to a club that pays its own way

Let's be honest with each other. Almost everything the club earns comes through the turnstile on a Saturday — and because we don't own Tithe Farm, that single gate is carrying the whole club on its back. A wet afternoon, a rough run, or a change behind the scenes, and the money dries up. That's why every year can feel like starting again with an empty tin.

This isn't about a magic figure. It's about building a club that earns in several different ways, so no single bad week sinks us — and so the volunteers stop forever firefighting. None of it is luck or quick. But clubs no bigger than us have done it.

Let's not dress it up. We got relegated — we're a Step 5 club that was a division higher not long ago. And every year since, the story's been the same three words: "no budget."

That excuse stops here. Relegation wasn't just bad luck — it's what happens when a club stops building the business behind the team. The flip side is the good bit: it's completely fixable, and the people who fix it are in this committee. Nobody's coming to save The Lane. We are the plan. So let's stop talking about what we can't do, and own what we can.

Why bother? The case for growing

Growing the income isn't greed — it's breathing room. A club earning from several places can keep its better players instead of losing them every summer, plan a season instead of surviving one, and becomes somewhere sponsors and young families want to be. It looks after the volunteers too: when the club isn't skint, nobody's begging for raffle prizes the night before a game. Stability is the prize — everything else here is just how you buy it.

🏆 The Wins Board — what we're bringing in

This is ours, and it's live for the whole committee. Every time you land something for The Lane — a board sold, ten lottery members signed, an event booked, a sponsor's yes — put your name on the board. Watch it grow. This is how we hold each other up, and how we prove "no budget" was always a choice.

⭐ Why Rayners Lane? (your pitch)

Before anyone hands money over — a sponsor, a local firm, a serious backer — they need to know why this club. Here's the case. It's a strong one.

A club with pedigree & ambition

The Lane has competed at Step 4 — the division above where we sit today. We were there, and we got relegated back to Step 5. That's not a track record to hide; handled right it's proof of what this club can reach. The mission is simple and it sells: climb back, and this time build the business underneath it so we actually stay there. People back ambition with history behind it. We have both.

90+ years & a town on the doorstep

Founded in 1933 — over nine decades serving Harrow, a London borough of around a quarter of a million people, one of the most diverse in the country. An FA Charter Standard community club with deep roots. That's real, local, loyal reach — exactly what a local business is paying to sit next to.

A head start nobody else at this level has

This portal. A Fan Zone with member cards, numbers and rewards. Live data, a sponsor hub, a ready-made social plan. Most clubs three divisions above us don't have this. It tells a sponsor you're professional and serious — and it gives you the audience and the numbers to prove your value.

🛠️ Tool — Generate your pitch

Pick who you're approaching and we'll write a ready-to-send pitch using the club's story. Tweak it, then copy.

Clubs no bigger than us who worked it out

Real examples, not theory. Some had advantages we don't — so here's the part we can actually copy.

⚽ Dulwich Hamlet — won on price & atmosphere

A seventh-tier club in south London that regularly draws crowds of over 3,000 — a thousand a game clear of anyone in their division. No fortune behind them. They won on keeping it genuinely affordable (some of the widest concessions in football, even a "pay your age" season ticket), getting loud on social media early, and making matchday an experience — proper food, a good bar, comedy nights.

What we copy: people turn up, and bring friends, for a warm, affordable, fun afternoon. The football is only half of it.
👕 Clapton CFC — an identity people wanted to wear

A small London non-league club that sold 2,500 shirts before they'd even played a competitive match — then 5,400+ pre-orders from around the world, 20,000+ kits since. They'd budgeted for 250. The trick: a shirt with a real story, sold on pre-order — made to order, so they never gambled on stock they hadn't sold.

What we copy: identity sells merch far beyond your own town — and pre-orders mean zero risk. We never buy a batch we haven't already sold.
📱 Hashtag United — built the audience first

Started online and grew to over 2 million followers and half a billion views — without owning a ground for years. That audience is the business: it brought deals with the likes of Adidas. They proved a non-league club's following is an asset sponsors pay real money to reach.

What we copy: we'll never have their millions, but the principle holds — a good, regular content following is something sponsors want. (See the social roadmap below.)
🤝 Worthing & South Shields — the town came back

Both reconnected a whole town with its club and now pull 2,000–3,000+. They went deep into the community — disability teams, walking football, dementia-friendly groups, youth foundations — and chased grants (Worthing raised close to £500,000 for pitch and floodlight upgrades).

What we copy: community work gives the town a reason to care and unlocks grant money that only exists for clubs doing good locally.
Every way to bring money in

The full menu. You won't do all of it — pick what fits your people. The tag shows roughly how hard each is to start.

The matchday machine
Ongoing

The engine we own. More people through the gate beats squeezing the few who come. Keep it affordable, then add a 50/50 draw, hot food, music and a proper welcome. Together a good matchday earns far more than the gate alone.

The 100 Club (recurring lottery)
Quick win

Members pay a few pounds a month for a monthly cash prize. Through a provider the club typically keeps around £6 of every £10 — a hundred members is roughly £600 a month, rain or shine. The steadiest money there is.

Sponsorship — the full menu
Legwork

Not just the shirt (and be loud about HDL & McCafferty's, who already back us). Offer a clear menu: match sponsor (a named game, ~£100–250), matchball sponsor (cheaper entry point, great for small firms), player sponsor (~£50–100 a player), kit / equipment sponsor (training wear, balls, bibs, the warm-up tops), programme ads, and pitch-side boards (~£350–500 a season each, our commercial workhorse). Sell it as marketing, not charity.

Investors & serious backers
Patient

A bigger ask with a bigger story — see "Approaching investors" below. The honest version: a local benefactor or business partner who buys into the climb back up. The ambition (Step 4 again) is what makes it an investment, not just a donation.

Merch — without the cashflow risk
Slow burn

Run pre-order drops (collect the money, then order) or print-on-demand — never buy stock you haven't sold. A "1933" retro shirt or a Yellow Army hoodie, opened for two weeks. Clapton turned this into their biggest single line.

Social media income (TikTok / YouTube)
Slow burn, real upside

Your content can earn directly — platform creator payments once you hit the thresholds, plus brand deals and merch driven by the audience. It's a long game, but it's free to start and compounds. Full roadmap in the next section.

Events
Bursts of cash

Quiz nights, race nights, a summer family fun day, a sportsman's dinner / presentation evening. Each pulls in money and new faces, and the bar wins too.

Community & grants
Paperwork

Honest truth: grants are applications and patience, not a button you press — and you won't win them all. But they're real and big. Football Foundation, the FA, Middlesex FA, the council and National Lottery back clubs doing good locally. Needs one organised person to own the forms.

📈 Where this gets us — the 3-year climb

Estimates, not promises — but grounded in what clubs like us actually do. The point is the shape: it compounds. Year one is graft for a modest return; by year three the recurring streams and the audience are doing the heavy lifting. These are figures the club can genuinely build toward.

Year 1 — Foundations
~£15k–25k

Get the 100 Club live (aim 80–100 members), sell the first 10–15 boards, run a tighter matchday with a 50/50 and food, and one or two events. Hard yards for a real return — and every brick laid here pays out again next year.

Year 2 — Momentum
~£25k–40k

The lottery renews and grows, boards fill out and renew, supporter memberships climb, the social following starts attracting content sponsors, and you land your first proper grant. Attendances tick up because the matchday's worth coming to.

Year 3 — Established
~£40k–60k+

Recurring income (lottery, boards, members) is a dependable floor that survives any bad run. Merch and content are earning. 📲 The social media money roadmap

Why this matters: an audience is an asset. It earns directly through the platforms, and it's what makes sponsors and merch work. It costs nothing but consistency — and a non-league club's honesty and characters are exactly what does well online.

Step 1 — Pick one flagship & post every week

Choose TikTok or YouTube as your main push (Instagram stays the shop window). Shoot vertical clips on a phone — goals, tunnel walk-outs, dressing room, fan chants, the gaffer's reaction. The Social tab has captions and 20 ready ideas. Consistency beats quality — one decent clip a week, every week, for months.

Step 2 — Hit the monetisation thresholds

Roughly (platforms change the rules, so check current ones): YouTube pays you once you reach about 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours (or ~10M Shorts views). TikTok's creator rewards need a few thousand followers and steady views. Getting there takes months of regular posting — treat it as a season-long project, not a quick flip.

Step 3 — Turn the audience into real money

Platform payouts are small at first — the real money is what the audience unlocks: a local business paying to sponsor your match-highlights series, merch drops to engaged followers, and a stronger number to wave at every sponsor ("we reach X thousand people a week"). That's the Hashtag United lesson at our scale.

The honest timeline: months, not weeks. But it's free, it compounds, and every follower makes every other money-stream on this page easier.
🏦 Approaching investors & sponsors — how to

"Investment" at our level rarely means a takeover. Realistically it's one of these — and each is achievable:

  • A local benefactor / patron — someone who loves the club and backs a specific goal (the climb, a facility, a season's playing budget).
  • A commercial partner — a bigger business that goes beyond a board: a season-long headline partnership with their name on everything.
  • Community shares / crowdfunding — many fans putting in a little for a cause they own (clubs like Lewes and FC United built this way).
  • A short-term loan for one project — repaid from the income it creates.
What they need to see

A plan (this playbook). Clean books they can trust (the Treasurer + Club Analytics — money in, money out, no mystery). A story (1933, Step 4 pedigree, the climb back). And their reason — a return, real exposure to a quarter-million-strong community, or a cause they're proud to put their name to.

How to actually do it

Warm introductions first — who do we already know? Lead with the one-page pitch (generate it above). Make the ask specific: "£X for Y, and here's exactly what you get." Meet in person, bring the chairman for the big ones, and follow up — most deals are won on the second conversation, not the first.
