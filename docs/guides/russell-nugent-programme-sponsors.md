# Russell's guide — Treasury, Programme, Marketing and Sponsorship

## What you see first
**Current programme** · programme readiness · **Sponsors** · sponsor artwork ·
marketing · safe finance summaries · sponsor expiry. Then **All Club Tools**.

## You and Darren
You have the **same access** but **separate accounts**. That is on purpose: the
club needs to be able to answer "who approved this?", and a shared login makes
that permanently unanswerable.

Either of you can pick up the other's unfinished work. Nothing is locked to one
person.

## The programme
Built automatically from what the club already holds — squad, table, committee,
sponsors. **Home matches only.** If we're away there is no programme, and that is
correct.

It publishes on matchday once **both** official team sheets are confirmed on FWP.
When it says it's waiting, it's waiting for FWP.

Once published, an edition never changes. An archived programme shows the club as
it was that day.

The legal footer — company name, number, registered address — is generated
automatically to meet FA rules. You don't type it and you can't get it wrong.

## Approving
Routine editions you can publish yourselves. Get a second pair of eyes for anything
unusual: a new sponsor commitment, anything naming a person critically, or a change
to the club's legal details.

That's a working rule, not bureaucracy. The routine edition is assembled from
sources the club already trusts.

## Sponsors
Records, logos, and where they appear on the website and in the programme. Sponsor
changes **do** reach the public website — check the badge.

## Money
**Matchday takings only** — what came through the gate and whether it reconciles.

**Bank details, invoices, payroll and tax records are not in the portal.** They
belong in proper accounting software. Please don't try to store them here; the
club's information files are readable by anyone who knows where to look.

## Correcting a mistake
Sponsor and programme content: change it and save again. A **published** programme
edition cannot be edited — that's what immutable means. If one is wrong, say so and
it can be republished.
